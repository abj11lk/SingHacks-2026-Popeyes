# Slide content — Wealth Intelligence

Product name: **North Bear** — "North" for direction/guidance (a direct echo
of the challenge brief's own "Challenge North Star" framing: *"help RMs
understand what matters, anticipate what may happen next"*), "Bear" for
Julius Baer. The pitch in the name itself: from portfolio *data* to advisory
*direction*.

Each section below maps to one slide. Copy/adapt directly — this is written
to be slide-ready, not prose to summarize further.

---

## 1. Problem statement (short — deliberately not the generic framing)

Every team got the same brief, so don't open with "portfolios are complex
and tools are descriptive" — everyone will say that. Open with the specific
gap instead:

> Priscilla Ong covers 20 clients across Singapore and Hong Kong. Her
> current tools show her *numbers*. They don't tell her that a client's bond
> loss is a rate story, not a mismanagement story — or that a "diversifying"
> structured product secretly references a stock he already owns — or that a
> redevelopment loan comes due before enough of the portfolio is actually
> liquid. **The gap isn't data. It's interpretation.**

---

## 2. What we chose to tackle

The brief explicitly says: go deep on 2-3 clients, not shallow across 20.
We took that literally. Three real clients, three real problems, from the
actual dataset:

- **Cheung Kwok Wing** (71, retired) — a bond portfolio down 7%, drawing
  income he can't outlive the recovery of. *(→ Explanation)*
- **Abdullah Al-Mansoori** — a structured product whose "worst-of basket"
  turns out to reference his own shipping business; an unresolved Strait of
  Hormuz situation he explicitly asked us to model. *(→ Risk + Scenario)*
- **Lau Chi Ming** — the same Hong Kong property bet spread across four
  different-looking wrappers, plus a financing deadline his portfolio can't
  actually meet from what's liquid. *(→ Recommendation)*

We built the capabilities these three clients actually needed, not a
checklist of every capability the brief mentions.

---

## 3. The solution

**North Bear** — an AI intelligence layer that sits between the portfolio data
and the RM: it explains what happened, flags what's hidden, stress-tests
what's unresolved, and proposes what to do next — with the RM approving
every single action.

Not a chatbot. No open text box. Every insight is a structured card the RM
reads, questions, and decides on — closer to a research analyst's memo than
a conversation.

---

## 4. How it works — three user scenarios

**Scenario A — "Why did my bond portfolio lose money?"**
Priscilla opens Cheung Kwok Wing's page. AUM is down 6.98%. One click on
Explanation: the loss is a *price* effect from rising 10-year yields (4.05%
→ 4.66%, cited from real market data) on bonds he still holds — not a sale,
not mismanagement. It connects this to his own words from a prior call
("I don't want to sell at a loss") and flags that his longest bond doesn't
mature until 2045 — waiting isn't a plan he can outlive at 71.

**Scenario B — "What if the situation in the Gulf changes?"**
Abdullah asked his RM this directly and she hadn't modelled it. One click on
Scenario: the system finds, on its own — never told the topic — that the
Strait of Hormuz closure is the one unresolved event still touching his
portfolio, because a structured note he holds references a shipping company
he *also* owns directly, which is *also* his own operating business. Same
bet, three times. It then projects both directions — escalation and
resolution — with real historical grounding, not guesses.

**Scenario C — "What should I actually do about this client?"**
Lau Chi Ming has ~53% of his portfolio in one property name across four
wrappers, and a HKD 60m financing deadline his liquid assets can't cover.
Recommendation proposes three concrete actions — increase an underweight
allocation, trim two positions breaching their concentration limit, draw a
credit facility instead of forcing a sale. Priscilla accepts one, edits the
wording on another, rejects the third. Every decision is permanently logged.

---

## 5. Key features (4)

1. **Explainable Attribution** — every portfolio move traced to a real,
   dated event or price move, never "the model just knows this." Separates
   "the market moved" from "you sold."
2. **Hidden Risk Look-Through** — surfaces concentration across
   differently-named wrappers and mandate breaches a standard report
   wouldn't connect.
3. **Scenario Stress-Testing** — identifies which real, unresolved
   situation actually matters to a client (without being told), and
   projects both directions honestly labelled as projection, not fact.
4. **Human-Governed Recommendations** — concrete, individually
   accept/edit/reject actions. Nothing executes itself; every decision is
   audited.
